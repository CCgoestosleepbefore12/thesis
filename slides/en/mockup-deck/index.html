§ 1 · Motivation

0.1 rad of encoder drift
breaks the assembly task.

A 0.1 rad (~5.7°) calibration offset shifts the end-effector pose by ~6.5 cm — and up to ~19 cm under severe bias (0.3 rad). Far beyond the tolerance of any precision manipulation task.

Current industrial practice: halt the line, recalibrate — throughput cost is non-trivial.

We adapt online — no production halt required.

§ 1 · Motivation · Encoder Bias on the Real Robot

Inject the bias on the real robot — the end-effector drifts

· Random J1 encoder bias b ~ U[−0.2, 0.2] rad injected via the B+D dual-injection points. The TCP drifts away from where the controller thinks it is — this is the few-centimeter drift, live on real hardware. The rest of the talk is about adapting to it without halting.

§ 2 · Problem Formulation

From MDP to POMDP — encoder bias as a hidden variable

Standard MDP
⟨ S, A, P, R, γ ⟩
Sstate space
Aaction space
P(s′|s,a)transition
R(s,a)reward
γdiscount
Agent observes s directly.
Policy π : S → A.
POMDP
⟨ S, A, P, R, Ω, O, γ ⟩
Ωobservation space
O(o|s)observation function
Agent sees o, not s. Hidden variable confounds s → o.
Policy π : Ω → A   or   π : B(s) → A.
Our setting
qmeas = qtrue + b,   b ∈ ℝ7 hidden
The agent never accesses qtrue directly. b is the unobservable confound — exactly the POMDP definition.

§ 3 · Method · Mechanism 1 · Observation Augmentation

Which observation channels carry information about b?

25D observation space (sim), split by bias contamination & task relevance.

22–24 Hand position phand Vision + depth (D455) ✓ no runtime safety
Without Real TCP

Agent cannot distinguish (q, b) from (q − δ, b + δ). The optimal policy degenerates into average compensation over the bias distribution — hedging across all b but mastering no specific b.

With Real TCP · disambiguating signal

Δx  =  FK(qtrue)  −  FK(q + b)  ≈  f(b)

lets the policy infer b implicitly from a single-step observation.

§ 3 · Method · Mechanism 2 · Random-Bias Training

Sample b at every episode — let the policy condition on it

Training protocol

At every episode reset:

bi  ∼  𝒰[ 0, bmax ],   for  i = 1, …, 7
  • b held constant within an episode.
  • bmax = 0.25 rad  (≈ 14°) — larger than the bias magnitudes surveyed for industrial robots; a deliberately aggressive stress test.
  • b is NOT fed to the policy as input — the policy must infer it implicitly from the 25D observation.
Domain Randomization (Tobin et al.)

Randomizes visual textures — redundant nuisance variables.

Policy should be invariant to the randomization.

Goal: learn invariance.

Our Random-Bias Training

Randomizes b — a hidden confound the policy must condition on.

Policy must output different actions for different b.

Goal: implicit identification & condition.

⇒ Optimization target: expected return over the b distribution, not worst-case (robust MDP).

§ 3 · Method · Network Architecture

One network — shared by task and backup policies, sim and real

· Frozen DINOv3-S backbone (22M params) · 768D shared feature · continuous actor + twin critic + discrete gripper critic. Both task and backup policies use this architecture; real-robot BC trains the actor only.
§ 3 · Method · Task Policy · Simulation

Task policy in simulation — HIL-SERL (RLPD)

Pipeline
  1. 50 human demos → offline demo buffer
  2. BC warmup — 500 steps, offline-only
  3. SAC online (RLPD) — replay mix 50% offline + 50% online
  4. Human keyboard intervention during online collection
  5. successful interventions → appended back to the offline buffer

Why simulation allows this: rollouts are cheap, throughput is high, and HIL intervention compensates for the sparse reward — online RL converges. The real robot cannot (see § 4 negative result).

§ 3 · Method · Task Policy · Real Robot

Task policy on the real robot — BC + HG-DAgger

Pipeline: Collect human demos → BC train (actor only) → HG-DAgger iterations — rollout → human takes over → add to buffer → retrain → repeat
§ 3 · Method · Backup Policy · Sim-only Training

Backup collision-avoidance — trained only in simulation, deployed zero-shot

Backup avoidance in simulation
How it is trained

Pure-simulation SAC + Domain Randomization — same network as the task policy. Reward / geometry evolution V1 → V3 → V3b; final V3b uses 5 link-spheres (link3 / 4 / 5 / 6 / hand), min distance. Sim hand-collision rate 3% — expected upper bound for real.

Why no real-robot fine-tuning?

Task policy needs HG-DAgger on the real robot — vision-heavy, large sim-real gap. Backup policy reasons over joint / Cartesian distances only — small gap, so zero-shot transfer works: load the V3b checkpoint directly.

⇒ Two policies, two training regimes — task adapts on the real robot, backup transfers zero-shot. Real-robot deployment follows next; the two are coordinated at runtime by the Supervisor FSM.

§ 4 · Simulation Results · HIL-SERL on the Real Robot — a Negative Result

Online RL fails on the real robot — the actor drifts the moment it unfreezes

What happened

Entering SAC online from the BC checkpoint: within 1–2 episodes after the actor unfreezes the policy diverges from BC — TCP flies to the workspace edge ≥ 3×/episode, gripper opens/closes nonstop. Multiple P0 fixes (HTTP retry, temperature floor, critic-only warmup, NaN skip, encoder freeze, …) — all effective in simulation, none fixed it on the real robot.

Leading hypothesis

The Q-surface is too flat. Under 50 demos + sparse reward + short episodes, the critic's Bellman-error surface is nearly indistinguishable across actions at a given state — once the actor unfreezes, its gradient direction is essentially arbitrary. HIL-SERL relies on high real-robot throughput to let the critic calibrate the Q-surface online; at 10 Hz / ≤ 30 s episodes that condition isn't met. HG-DAgger doesn't depend on a critic — improvement comes directly from the human correction gradient.

Alternatives (not ruled out)
  • keyboard reward-label noise → contaminates Q-target
  • BC (MLE) vs SAC (max-Q) actor / critic mismatch
  • gRPC tens-of-ms jitter amplifies critic instability
⇒ Recorded as a methodological negative result — and the direct reason the real-robot path uses BC + HG-DAgger, not online RL. A warning for anyone considering HIL-SERL at this throughput / data scale.

§ 6 · Conclusion

Three contributions — theory, method, empirics

I
Theory

POMDP formulation of encoder bias — one-source-three-effect causal chain; two identifiability propositions (Prop. 1 unidentifiable from proprioception alone / Prop. 2 approx. identifiable with an external position signal). "Identifiable ≠ solvable" → random-bias training is necessary.

II
Method

task ⊕ backup × sim/real dual-path framework. Shared 25D observation + random-bias training + frozen DINOv3 backbone. HIL-SERL (sim) / BC + HG-DAgger (real). Hierarchical Supervisor — 3-state FSM (TASK / BACKUP / HOMING).

III
Empirics

4-column train × test bias success-rate matrix on 3 real-robot tasks (pickup / wipe / pickandplace). Plus a methodological negative result — HIL-SERL fails on the real robot under sparse-reward / 10 Hz / N = 1.

Limitations
Real-robot bias closed loop not completed — uses the tcp_true privileged channel, not the implicit identification of Prop. 2.
Bias types limited — J1 single joint, episode-constant; no thermal drift or multi-joint coupling.
Single platform (Franka Panda) + 3 kitchen tasks; the paradigm-choice principle needs more platforms.
HIL-SERL negative result: N = 1, 10 Hz; "Q-surface flat" is the leading hypothesis, not confirmed.
§ 6 · Future Work

Future work — closing the real-robot identifiability loop

1
Two-step ablation on the real robot most direct

(i) replace tcp_true with tcp_biased = FK(q+b) → should fail (Prop. 1). (ii) add the encoder path q+b → should recover (Prop. 2). Isomorphic to the sim H2/H3 path.

2
Multi-joint / time-varying bias & other faults

Coupled multi-joint bias, thermal drift, encoder noise, joint jam, actuator degradation — map the framework's range of applicability.

3
Long-horizon orchestration via foundation models

Currently pickup / wipe / pickandplace are three independent short-horizon tasks. Use a VLM / LLM as a high-level planner to chain them into one long-horizon task — e.g. "clean the desk" = pickup → wipe → place-back. The bias-robust short-horizon policies become primitives in a foundation-model orchestration loop.
